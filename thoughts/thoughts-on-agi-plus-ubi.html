Life 3.0, and thoughts on AGI + UBI

July 2026 · 3 min read

Wow, 2026 is halfway over, and it still feels like 2020 was just yesterday. Time definitely flies, and the past 6 years have been a wild ride.

I want to talk about what's going on in my head. I've been reading Max Tegmark's Life 3.0, and given the current state of AI (Fable 5 getting banned and resurrected again, GPT-5.5 also under government scrutiny), it feels like all the AI hype, progress, and consequences are very, very real.

The one piece I've been thinking about for the past 2 days (after finishing chapter 3 of Life 3.0) is human involvement and consequences. Basically, if we achieve AGI/ASI in reality, there would be no work left for humans to do, or maybe only minimal amounts of work left. According to Max himself, Life 3.0 is intelligence capable of creating its own software as well as hardware with excellent dexterity. At that point, we'd witness an intelligence (life) explosion, and we'd be living in a world almost operated (and maybe even not governed) by robots. Yes, Wall-E would possibly become a reality.

But then I look at all my co-workers, and the relationships I have at work and because of work. I think about the satisfaction of finishing a complex project, of bringing something meaningful and useful to a user's life. I think about the mental sanity work brings, for the most part. Work fills at least 1/3rd of our lives at this point. And with a hardware + software intelligence explosion, this might get stripped away, given that the majority of humanity might not have anything left to do, since robots and intelligent software will be capable of doing the same tasks much more efficiently (both operationally and financially). This could also help our societies move toward abundance, given that there would be plenty of $$'s generated by automated work and efficiency.

But then who gets to keep that wealth? The top dawgs? The CEOs? The ultra-rich? The government? I don't think any of us really knows, since this is the future we're trying to predict. But yes, this is something that is concerning, and we definitely should not take it lightly by any means. There's also speculation that this could create a massive divide between the poor, the working class, and the ultra-rich, and that the inequality gap will grow larger and larger, even if overall standards of living go up.

The concept of UBI (universal basic income) is probably already familiar to all the AI nerds out there, but if it's new to you, the TLDR is: all the wealth generated by superintelligence would be redistributed to the general public, since ASI should be programmed by default to serve humankind. Bernie Sanders recently introduced the American AI Sovereign Wealth Fund Act, which essentially proposes a 50% public ownership stake in major AI companies through a one-time stock tax, which essentially states that the general public in the US would directly benefit from the revenue and profits of privately controlled AI mega-corps. The Trump administration also seems very much aligned with this idea, in fact, OpenAI just floated a proposal today to cede a 5% equity stake to the U.S. government. I hope there are people aware of this, and who actually care enough, so that coming generations can thrive and reap the benefits of humanity's amazing leaps in AI development alongside a fairer distribution of wealth.

It's almost (yes, almost) impossible to predict the future. But given the current progress in AI development and its applications, I have faith that the right people are working hard to make the right choices, and that no harm will come to regular, everyday people because of AI.

Well, that's all for today! I'm looking forward to consistently writing about things I'm passionate about, and I hope you got some value out of this :)
